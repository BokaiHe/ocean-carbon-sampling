01 · Introduction

The ocean absorbs our CO₂.
We measure only a sliver of it.

≈ 1/4of human CO₂ emissions is taken up by the ocean each yearSource: Global Carbon Budget
3.4%of monthly 1° ocean grid cells had a ship measurement in 2014
5,000samples per experiment — only where they go changes

Machine learning turns sparse measurements into complete maps of ocean CO₂. If the measurements are clustered, do the maps get worse — and where should the next ones go?

03 · Method

A simulated ocean
as the answer key.

The real ocean has no complete answer. So we use an Earth-system-model ocean as truth, hide part of it, and test how well each sampling rule recovers it.

  1. TruthIPSL-CM6A-LR gives complete monthly surface pCO₂
  2. HideSet aside 20% of month-cells as one shared test set
  3. SamplePick 5,000 month-cells with one rule
  4. LearnOne gradient-boosting model fills the map from SST, salinity, position and month
  5. ScoreCompare with the hidden truth across 20 paired seeds

Main comparison: south of 60°N · connected ocean · area-weighted · 2005, 2010, 2014 · 20 paired seeds

06 · Conclusion

In these tests, historical-density sampling costs accuracy.
Both addition rules improve reconstruction.

+27%

Where we measure matters

At the same 5,000 samples, allocating by historical observation density gives higher MAE than random sampling — in every year tested.

 RMSE ↓

Spreading out is not a cure-all

Even coverage trims the largest errors, mostly near coasts and in marginal seas, while typical error barely moves.

 −2.9

Two ways to add observations

In the 2005 open-ocean test, both rules for adding 1,000 observations reduce RMSE. Their gains over the existing network exceed the small difference between the two rules.

Sampling location matters, but these tests do not identify an optimal deployment. Every result is conditional on one simulated ocean and one learner; benefits for the real network remain unverified.

07 · Limits

What this study
cannot tell you.

  • One Earth-system-model ocean
  • One learner, a few predictors
  • Idealised observations, no measurement error
  • Grid cells, not real ship routes, cost or feasibility
  • South of 60°N only
  • Augmentation tested in 2005 only
