01 / The question

A ship sees a route.
We need the whole picture.

Measurements tell us about a few places. Models help fill the gaps. Does choosing different places give us a better picture of ocean CO₂?

What are we measuring?

Reconstruction means estimating the missing parts of a map from scattered measurements. The quantity here is pCO₂, a measure of CO₂ pressure in seawater—not a direct map of carbon uptake. Shipboard measurements · NOAA ↗

The experiment

5,000 measurements. Three ways to place them. What changes?

02 / A controlled test

Hide the answer.
Then try to recover it.

A simulated ocean lets us check predictions against values we already know.

  1. 1. Keep the answersStart with monthly CO₂ maps from an ocean simulation.
  2. 2. Set a test asideHide the same places and months from all three sampling plans.
  3. 3. Choose 5,000From the remaining pool, choose measurements using each plan.
  4. 4. Fill the gapsTrain the same type of model to predict the hidden values.
  5. 5. Check the errorCompare predictions with the answers we kept. Smaller errors are better.

RandomGive each eligible location-month an equal chance.

Historical-densityPrefer places and months with more past SOCAT observations—not actual ship routes.

CoverageSpread measurements across space and months by prioritizing underrepresented blocks.

One IPSL-CM6A-LR simulation · one fixed HistGradientBoosting learner · 2005, 2010, 2014 · 20 paired seeds. Each observation is one location in one month, not one ship or one voyage.

What makes the comparison fair—and what does it not test?

Holdout happens before training. Strategies share the same test set, sample count, model settings and paired seeds. The primary evaluation hides scattered month-cells; the stress test hides entire blocks. In the primary and aligned stress-test comparisons, both sampling and evaluation are restricted to <60°N and errors are area-weighted.

The simulated observations have no added measurement error. The experiment compares idealized placement rules, not feasible ship routes, deployment costs or a universally best observing network. The full-domain supporting maps below use a different, clearly labelled scope.

The white land layer is drawn above all data symbols. It can cover coastal block centres and inland-water values because the simplified land basemap does not distinguish every water body. This is a cartographic overlay, not a scientific exclusion mask or a correction to the experimental domain. Covered values remain in downloads and coordinate inspection; visible dots alone do not sum to the full sample count. The inland-water eligibility issue remains under review.

The globe uses real geographic coordinates, not real ship tracks. Dots summarize selected observations at 5° × 10° block centres from one seed. Historical-density draws month-cells without replacement using SOCAT 1990–2004 count weights; it does not replay cruises or repeat lines. Random gives each candidate equal probability; coverage prioritizes underrepresented month-space blocks. No route or marginal benefit of adding a sample is inferred.

Signed bias: why the interpretation changed

What changed our interpretation

A strong-looking bias. A narrower conclusion.

Whole-block stress test only. Weighting and domain choices shrink the signed offset—not evidence of a universal bias.

Primary evaluation, stress test & replication

Hidden-cell evaluation reserves about 20% of month-cells within each month before sampling. One fixed set per year is shared across strategies and 20 paired seeds. Whole-block evaluation excludes all months in 20° × 10° blocks, using five checkerboard folds without a buffer.

Three years share one ESM run. The three primary year means and 15 stress-test year–fold means are descriptive units, not independent Earth-system replicates. No formal whole-block significance test is claimed. Neither protocol is full-field evaluation after unrestricted sampling.

The September 2026 presentation change foregrounds the existing hidden-cell analysis. It is a post-analysis reframing, not a newly preregistered protocol. Differences are computed before rounding.

Full evaluation scope & source notes ↗
Four design boundaries & not yet supported
  • Idealized observations. Monthly model truth has no added measurement or point-to-grid representation error. Predictors come from the same simulation; real-world rankings may differ.
  • Fixed learner. HistGradientBoosting settings do not change with count or strategy. Count effects are conditional on the learner, not geometry alone.
  • Limited inputs. SST, salinity, latitude, cyclic longitude, year and cyclic month; no MLD, chlorophyll or atmospheric CO₂. One ESM member, no feature-ablation claim.
  • Separate yearly fits. All 12 months fit jointly within each year. Interannual trends and decadal carbon-sink variability are not tested.

Cross-learner and cross-ESM robustness, noisy observations, full-field recovery, flux integration, sea-ice-aware access and feasible deployment plans remain not yet supported.

SOCAT source & archived observations

Historical-density uses 1990–2004 observation counts from Bakker et al. (2025), SOCAT v2025, not actual cruise trajectories. Observed fCO₂ values are not used as the OSSE truth.

The former real-observation globe is archived, not loaded on this page. Its monthly values and provenance (JSON) remain available. These are grid-cell centres, not exact ship positions, and this view is not the historical-density sampling mask. Monthly colours used per-cruise-weighted fCO₂; fCO₂ is fugacity, not dissolved-carbon concentration. An average of available months is not a complete annual mean.
